## <a name="tactics"></a>7. WINNING TACTICS

### What Makes Applications Win

**1. Clarity Beats Complexity**
- Bad: "We leverage synergistic paradigms for social capital formation"
- Good: "We connect lonely young professionals through dinner parties"

**2. Show, Don't Tell**
- Bad: "We have strong traction"
- Good: "15 test dinners, 85% attendance, 92% would-recommend"

**3. Problem First, Solution Second**
- Bad: "We built an AI-powered platform"
- Good: "61% of young Africans feel lonely. We fix this with dinners"

**4. Personal Story Creates Connection**
- Bad: "Market research shows demand"
- Good: "As a Scholar, I moved to Lagos and felt alone. I built this to solve it"

**5. Realistic Beats Ambitious**
- Bad: "10,000 users in 6 months"
- Good: "400 people through 50 dinners, validating $6/seat economics"

### Red Flags That Kill Applications

❌ **"No Competitors"** → Shows no market research
✅ Fix: "While Meetup exists, we differentiate through trust & safety"

❌ **Vague Use of Funds** → "Marketing and development"
✅ Fix: "Product: $4K, Validation: $3.5K, Operations: $2.5K..."

❌ **Technology-First** → "React, Next.js, PostgreSQL..."
✅ Fix: "Our platform ensures safety through identity verification"

❌ **Ignoring Risks** → "Our plan is foolproof"
✅ Fix: "Biggest risk is low attendance. We mitigate with commitment fees"

❌ **Solo with No Support** → "I'm doing everything myself"
✅ Fix: "I'm technical founder. Using funds to hire community manager"

### Psychological Tactics

**1. Inevitable Future Frame**
- Instead of: "We hope to connect people"
- Say: "Urban loneliness is solvable. DineWithMe is how we solve it"

**2. Already Happening Frame**
- Instead of: "We plan to launch"
- Say: "We're launching. 3 partnerships in development, 50 on waitlist"

**3. Specific Numbers**
- Instead of: "Many people feel lonely"
- Say: "61% of young professionals (WHO, 2024)"

**4. We Not I**
- Instead of: "I'm building a platform"
- Say: "We're building a movement with restaurant partners and hosts"

**5. Proof of Concept**
- Instead of: "We believe this will work"
- Say: "We've validated with 15 dinners. 85% attendance. Ready to scale"

### Writing Style

**Use Active Voice**
- Passive: "Dinners are hosted by partners"
- Active: "Partners host dinners"

**Use Short Sentences**
- Long: "We facilitate meaningful social connections through curated dining experiences that bring together young professionals"
- Short: "We connect lonely young professionals through dinner parties"

**Use Concrete Language**
- Abstract: "We create value through engagement"
- Concrete: "We help people make friends over dinner"

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Burying the Lead**
- Start with the problem (loneliness)
- Then your solution (dinners)
- Then why different (trust & safety)
- Then traction (75% MVP)
- Then the ask ($15K)

**2. Jargon Overload**
- Bad: "Disrupting social discovery vertical through B2B2C marketplace"
- Good: "Partner with restaurants to host dinners that help people make friends"

**3. Underselling Progress**
- Bad: "We've just started"
- Good: "75% MVP complete: payments, trust & safety, booking engine"

**4. Forgetting "So What?"**
- Bad: "Mobile-responsive design"
- Good: "Mobile booking increases conversion 40%"

**5. No Human Element**
- Bad: "Platform processes transactions efficiently"
- Good: "When Sarah moved to Nairobi, she knew no one. After 3 dinners, she has 8 friends"
